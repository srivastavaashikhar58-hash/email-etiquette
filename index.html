01 | THE BASICS

Why email etiquette matters

An email is often the first impression you make in an academic or professional setting.

💡

Clear

The reader quickly understands your purpose.

🤝

Respectful

The tone shows courtesy and consideration.

🎯

Effective

The message leads to the right next step.

  • ✓ Creates a professional and trustworthy image
  • ✓ Makes information easier to understand
  • ✓ Saves time and reduces misunderstandings
  • ✓ Builds positive relationships with others
02 | EMAIL ANATOMY

The structure of a professional email

1

Subject Line

A short, specific summary of the topic.

2

Greeting

Use an appropriate name or professional greeting.

3

Opening

State why you are writing.

4

Main Message

Give the necessary details in a logical order.

5

Closing / Action

Mention the next step or request, if needed.

6

Sign-off

End politely and include your name.

03 | START WELL

Subject lines, greetings and tone

❌ Weak Subject

"Hello" or "Important" — too vague to help the reader.

✅ Better Subject

"Request for Leave – 12 November" — clear and specific.

Choose a greeting that fits

  • Formal Dear Professor Sharma,
  • Neutral Hello Priya,
  • Team Hello everyone,

Keep the tone professional

  • ✔ Be polite, calm and direct
  • ✘ Avoid slang and unnecessary emojis
  • ✘ Do not write in ALL CAPITAL LETTERS
04 | THE MESSAGE

Write clearly and concisely

🔍

Keep it focused

One email should have a clear purpose. Remove details the reader does not need.

📖

Make it readable

Use short paragraphs, simple words and bullet points for multiple items.

📌

Be specific

Include dates, deadlines, names and requested actions where relevant.

A useful message pattern

Purpose Why am I writing?
→
Context What does the reader need to know?
→
Action What should happen next?
05 | PRESENTATION

Formatting and language essentials

✅ Do

  • Use a readable font and consistent formatting
  • Break long messages into short paragraphs
  • Use bullets or numbering for lists
  • Check spelling, grammar and punctuation
  • Use bold sparingly to highlight key information

❌ Don't

  • Use decorative fonts and excessive colours
  • Write like a text message
✅ Professional

"Could you please review the attached file?"

❌ Too Casual

"Heyyy check this out ASAP!!!"

Aim for friendly, not overly familiar.

06 | EMAIL TOOLS

Recipients, CC, BCC and attachments

📨

To

People expected to read or act on the email.

👁

CC

People kept informed; their addresses are visible.

🔒

BCC

Recipients are hidden from other recipients.

Before you press Send

  • Check that each recipient really needs the message.
  • Attach the correct file and mention the attachment in the email.
  • Use a clear file name, such as Project_Report_Nov2026.pdf.
  • Double-check recipient addresses, especially when sharing sensitive information.
07 | PUT IT INTO PRACTICE

Example: a professional email

Email
Subject: Request for an Extension – Communication Skills Assignment
To: Professor Sharma

Dear Professor Sharma,

I am writing to request a two-day extension for the Communication Skills assignment due on 5 November. I need a little more time to complete the final review and ensure that the work is accurate.

Would it be possible to submit the assignment by 7 November? I understand if this is not possible and appreciate your consideration.

Thank you for your time.

Regards,
Aarav

Clear subject Respectful tone Specific request Polite closing
08 | QUICK CHECK

Common email mistakes to avoid

⏰

Replying too late

Acknowledge important messages promptly.

📢

Reply All unnecessarily

Use Reply All only when everyone needs the response.

🔎

Sending without proofreading

Review names, dates, tone and attachments.

❓

Writing an unclear request

State exactly what you need and by when.

😤

Using emotional language

Pause and rewrite calmly before sending.
